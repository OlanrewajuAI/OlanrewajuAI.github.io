DisruptBench

Most agent evaluations struggle to say what the right answer was. In this domain we know it: my thesis optimisation model gives the provably optimal plan for every disruption scenario. That makes it possible to measure exactly how far an AI agent's plan falls from optimal, and whether it breaks hard physical constraints while sounding confident.

Step 1, done: the thesis MILP rebuilt in Python (PuLP/HiGHS): 5 crude fields, 4 refineries, 7 depots, 3 products, pipeline vs. truck. All 8 thesis scenarios solved to proven optimality with zero constraint violations. Next: a plan checker, then frontier-model agents tested with and without self-critique and solver access.

Demand forecasting with ML

I compared ANN, SVM, linear regression and decision trees on daily truck-out data from regulators (NMDPRA, NUPRC, NBS). ANN and SVM performed best. State-level errors showed failures the aggregate scores hid.

Two-hedging-point production policy

A continuous-flow model of refinery production. I extended the classic hedging-point buffer policy to hold two thresholds at once: one for energy security under disruption, one for limits on emissions and waste.

Multi-objective optimisation under risk

A MILP trading off cost, profit and service level with probabilistic pipeline vandalism and truck accidents. It was tested on eight scenarios, from a Niger Delta supply cut to refinery under-capacity.
